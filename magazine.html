The Advert Magazine

Typography II • UMass Lowell

A magazine based on the history & strategies of advertising, where the design of each article matches the style of the related advertisement. I aimed to break away from the usual consistency & repetitiveness of magazines and incorporated my own photography in the process.

Article 01

The Ad That Changed Advertising

The opening feature tells the story of DDB’s “Think Small” campaign for Volkswagen and the people behind it: Bill Bernbach, copywriter Julian Koenig and art director Helmut Krone.

The layout follows the rules Krone set for the original ads. It uses Futura, plenty of white space, three-column body copy and black-and-white images with a single red accent. Portraits and pull quotes break up the long read.

Article 02

Why ‘Got Milk?’ Is One of the Greatest Ad Campaigns of All Time

This article covers how “Got Milk?” grew from a California dairy campaign into a national icon, and why milk sales kept falling despite the campaign’s fame.

The article sits is weaved through a pattern of milk cartons, which also feature Got Milk? ads to illustrate the scale of the campaign. The headline is set in the same type as the Got Milk? wordmark.

Article 03

Paula Scher on Creating
Brand Identities

An interview with Paula Scher about her process and her identity work for New York’s cultural institutions, including The Public Theater.

The design borrows Scher’s own visual language. Oversized type runs in different weights and directions over solid blocks of blue, orange and black. In the Q&A spreads, large serif Q and A letterforms frame each answer, and her Public Theater posters appear alongside.

Article 04

All Star Ads: Visuals That
Set the Standard

A visual roundup of standout print ads from brands including Braun, Rimowa, Coca-Cola, McDonald’s, Nike, IKEA, Nescafé, Pepsi, DoorDash and Mitsubishi.

Instead of a clean gallery grid, each ad is ripped into a black page with torn-paper edges, like posters layered on a wall. The distressed headline type sets the same raw tone.
